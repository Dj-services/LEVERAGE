People-reading game · motives and deception

Leverage

Every person wants something. Read the clues, figure out what drives them, then use it to get what you need. Anyone might lie to you about what they want.

How to play: Read people

Goal: Get 8 people to give you what you need. Each correct read scores points, up to 800 total.

Each target

  1. Look at "What you notice." These clues are free and never lie, but on medium and hard targets some of them are random noise.
  2. Ask questions if you need to. Each one costs 20 points. A read is worth 100, minus 20 per question asked (never less than 40).
  3. Pick your approach. Choose the one that matches what the person really wants.

Liars

Anyone after the first two targets might lie. A liar's answers pretend to be a different motive. There are two ways to catch them: a detail in the room that contradicts their act (a "money" person who never checks an open cash drawer), and one answer where they slip and show the real motive.

Two-step targets

Targets 4 and 7 are coworkers of the next person. Read the coworker correctly and they give you a real clue about the next target. Read them wrong and you go in with nothing.

Difficulty

Targets 1 to 3 are easy, with 3 real clues. Targets 4 to 7 are medium, with 2 real clues plus noise. Target 8 is hard, with 1 subtle clue hidden in noise.

Losing and ranks

Three wrong reads blows your cover and ends the game. Ranks: Untouchable 680+, Sharp reader 520+, Getting there 360+, Too trusting below that.

How to play: Cover story

Goal: Get through 9 questions from the detective with fewer than 5 suspicion marks.

Setup

  1. Pick an answer for each part of your alibi, or hit Random story.
  2. Memorize it. It disappears once the interrogation starts.

Question types

  • Direct: "Where were you?" Pick the answer from your story.
  • Repeats: The same question asked again in different words. Your answer has to match.
  • Checks: The detective states a detail, like "So Ray was with you the whole time?" Sometimes it's true and sometimes it's a trap. Say yes only if it matches your story.
  • Evidence: A camera caught your car near the scene. Pick the explanation that fits how you said you got there. Never confess.

Tips

Simple stories are easier to remember. When the detective states a detail, compare it to your story before you agree. Answer fast, but not without thinking.
